How to Check a Media Link Before Sharing It

A rights-aware checklist for video, music and live streams

A link working in a browser does not automatically mean it is suitable to add to a public entertainment catalogue. Before a video, song or live stream is shared with other people, check both the source and the permission to use it. This simple review can prevent broken entries, misleading listings and copyright problems.

1. Identify who operates the source

Look for the official website or channel of the broadcaster, creator, label, distributor or hosting service. Be cautious when a link comes from an unfamiliar file host, a copied playlist or a page that does not identify the publisher. A familiar logo or title alone is not proof that a source is official.

2. Check the permission, not just the playback

Ask what the licence or permission actually allows. Permission to watch a video is not necessarily permission to embed it, redistribute the file, make it available to the public or monetize access to it. Public-domain status, open licences and direct written permission can provide different rights, and each has conditions that should be read carefully.

3. Read the terms for the exact item

Do not assume every item on a platform has the same licence. Check the individual film, recording, image or stream. Some services allow embedding but prohibit downloading; others allow reuse only with attribution or for non-commercial purposes. Keep a record of the source page and the applicable permission when you are responsible for publishing the entry.

4. Confirm that the link points to the right item

Open the URL in a private browser tab or a separate device. Confirm that it leads to the intended title rather than a search page, login screen, expired file or unrelated redirect. Check that the title, creator, artwork and description match the source. Avoid shortened URLs when the final destination is unclear.

5. Test the experience on mobile

Many visitors use phones. Check whether the page loads, whether the player controls are usable and whether the source opens without unexpected pop-ups or forced downloads. A link that works only after several redirects may be a poor experience even if it eventually reaches the content.

6. Do not bypass access controls

Do not add links that defeat subscriptions, paywalls, geographic restrictions, authentication or other access controls. Do not share private playlists, credentials or links intended for a limited audience. If the provider says a stream is not available for public redistribution, leave it out.

7. Keep listings accurate and respond to reports

Use a clear title, accurate content type and a description that does not promise more than the source provides. Recheck entries periodically because streams can move or disappear. If a rights holder reports a problem, pause the listing while the report is reviewed and use the site's published contact process.

A final pre-publication check

  • I can identify the original publisher or rights holder.
  • I have verified the licence or permission for the intended use.
  • The link points to the correct, publicly accessible item.
  • The title and description are accurate.
  • The link does not bypass payment, login or other access controls.
  • The mobile experience is safe and understandable.

If any answer is uncertain, do not publish the link until it has been checked.
